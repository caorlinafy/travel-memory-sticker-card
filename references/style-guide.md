# Visual style guide

## Core language

Record a place through selective memory rather than descriptive realism. Combine modern flat composition with tactile analog material: large matte gouache or cut-paper shapes, fine uniform paper tooth, awkward hand-made contours, restrained accents, and quiet negative space.

## Shape and material

- Make the scene read first as 3–6 large masses. Keep the dominant quiet field broad and calm.
- Build scenery from chunky irregular silhouettes and layered planes. Compress detailed vegetation into 1–3 tonal clusters; reduce terrain, buildings, and crowds to a few decisive shapes.
- Use opaque gouache, cut-paper, risograph, or screen-print-like fills with soft density variation, chalky edges, and light hand-cut misregistration. Apply one fine monochromatic paper grain consistently across image and stickers.
- Do not create marker bands, watercolor diffusion, wet brush edges, repeated linework, pebble dots, or intricate texture.

## Color and typography

- Derive 5–8 color families from the source. Favor dusty midtones and restrained contrast; use one or two crisp source-derived accents only.
- Use flat colored shadows, no gradients. Keep black sparse.
- Set exactly three short English keywords once in a small, quiet footer beneath the left image, separated by centered dots. Use muted ink and let the exposed paper carry them without a box or label.

## Card composition

- Keep a continuous warm-paper border around the entire card.
- Leave the left image completely unframed and visually dominant. Reserve only a shallow exposed-paper strip beneath it for the keyword footer.
- Treat the chosen three-to-six right-column stickers as distinct memory fragments, not a catalog: use uneven scale, relaxed spacing, thick warm-white cut edges, and subtle flat shadows. Do not repeat a subject in another crop or invent structure absent from the source.

## Avoid

- Photographic patches, painterly photo filters, lens blur, film grain, or realistic texture.
- Visible marker strokes, transparent watercolor washes, wet blooms, soft gradients, glossy 3D, or smooth vector precision.
- Detailed faces, anatomy, fabric folds, leaves, branches, vehicle mechanics, geology, or decorative filler.
- Titles, captions, dates, postal marks, watermarks, signatures, and all source text except one chosen landmark anchor.
